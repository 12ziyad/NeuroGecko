# NeuroGecko: From Prototype to Digital Animal — Final Strategy

*Prepared for the owner of github.com/12ziyad/NeuroGecko (local: C:\Users\ziyad\GeckoBrain). Date: 2026-09-04. Constraints taken as given: one part-time developer, CPU-only Windows laptop, $500 AWS credit, 12 weeks.*

This document merges the strategy that won two of three review lenses (**Kinematics-First**: make the body move like a filmed leopard gecko) with the parts of the two runners-up that the judges rated strongest (the **living world and cricket prey** from Ecology-First; the **basal-ganglia selector, drive-reduction reward, tectal vision front-end and code-enforced no-cheat boundary** from Region-Structured). Every gate, timeline and compute number the judges flagged as unrealistic has been rewritten, and every number was then checked against its primary source. Where a number is a proxy from another species, or derived rather than measured, it is labelled as one.

---

## 1. The honest picture

Today NeuroGecko is a good body wearing a prototype nervous system. The body (`morphology/gecko_body_r.xml`) is measurement-based, has the right physics settings (0.0008 s step, implicitfast, elliptic friction cones, dense Jacobian at nv = 38, calibrated foot-contact threshold 0.0564), and already carries an animal-like sensor suite: a trunk IMU site with gyro/accelerometer/velocimeter, four foot and two belly touch sensors, a `nose_tip` site, and a 64x64 `head_cam` (83 sensors in total, all of them supported by MuJoCo's GPU backend). Everything above the body is hand-designed rather than measured: the walker is a fixed-frequency central pattern generator (a CPG is a hand-written rhythm generator; `envs/cpg_residual_controller.py`, `FREQ_HZ = 1.1888`) plus a PPO correction capped at 0.25 with the front-limb lift locked (`lock_front_lift=True`), walking at 0.11 m/s — below the slowest level speed measured in real leopard geckos (0.2-1.1 m/s, Zaaf et al. 2001), with turning and speed that the brain cannot modulate. The "world" is one static, saturated green sphere (`envs/gecko_brain_env.py`, `food_radius=0.035`) eaten by proximity (`eat_radius=0.10`); the drive system (`brain/drives.py`) is a hand-tuned ODE whose key variable, `target_interest = 0.70·hunger + 0.25·curiosity − 0.35·fear`, is a hand-made value function; behaviour selection is an if-chain (REST/EXPLORE/SEARCH_FOOD/APPROACH_FOOD/EAT); search is scripted; the runtime observation dictionary still contains a `"privileged"` key (`_privileged_vector()`, lines 343-366) that reads the food position from the world; and the trained brain weights were lost with an AWS instance. No leopard gecko has a connectome, no gecko electrophysiology exists, and no public gecko kinematics dataset exists — so "real" can only mean two things here: the body reproduces measured gecko motion, and the animal's behaviour matches measured gecko ethology through senses the gecko actually has. Neither is true yet.

## 2. The strategy in one paragraph

The thesis: **an animal is real to the extent that its body moves like the animal and its behaviour comes from the animal's own senses and physiology; the brain is only as real as the body it drives and the world it lives in.** Between 2024 and 2026 every credible whole-animal model without a connectome converged on the same recipe — the virtual rodent (Aldarondo et al., Nature 2024), MIMIC-MJX (2025-26), flybody (Nature 2025) and NeuroMechFly v2 (Nature Methods 2024): (1) track real animal kinematics and force the simulated body to reproduce them, (2) wrap the body in a spine organised as biology says (descending drive → CPG → reflexes), (3) mount the animal's own sensor suite, and (4) validate by behaviour, because there is nothing else to validate against. The virtual rodent then showed that a controller trained this way predicts real motor-circuit activity better than any kinematic feature — the strongest evidence that this route is a genuine step toward neural realism without a wiring diagram. So we do exactly that: film a real leopard gecko, retarget the tracks onto the existing body, train the walker by imitation (DeepMimic tracking, then an AMP "style" reward) with a bilateral descending drive as the command; in parallel, on the laptop, replace the fake world with cricket prey that escape the way measured crickets escape, replace the if-chain with a published basal-ganglia selector fed by drive-reduction reward, replace the scripted search with a tectum-like saliency map, and enforce in code that no privileged information reaches the animal at runtime. The two tracks meet behind a single descending-drive interface, so the world and brain work are never blocked on the walker. This is the best next step toward connectome-level realism because it produces the two things a future gecko connectome (if one ever exists) would need to plug into: a body proven to move like the species, and a sensory-behavioural test battery that says what "correct" looks like.

## 5. The 3-month roadmap

Two tracks run in parallel. **Track A (walker)** needs the gecko footage and the GPU; **Track B (world + brain)** runs on the laptop and CPU spot boxes and is never blocked by Track A. They meet in Phase 3 behind the descending-drive interface.

**Hard rules for the 12 weeks:** no changes to the frozen V4.5B policy or its CPG; every sensory addition is ordered by realism-per-hour and may be cut; every proxy parameter carries a source and a confidence; every training script checkpoints to S3; no GPU hours are bought before the Phase 0 float32 check has been run.

### Phase 0 — Seal, measure, secure the data (Weeks 1-2) — budget ~$5

Track A:
- Secure access to a live leopard gecko (own, borrowed, breeder, pet shop, local herp society). **If no animal is confirmed by the end of week 2, Track A switches to Plan B** (below).
- Build the runway (about 1.0 × 0.13 m, sandpaper, walls — the Jagnandan & Higham trackway) with a 45° mirror giving dorsal + lateral views in one frame (the Higham lab used exactly this mirror, but with two synchronised cameras and DLTdv, not Anipose); consider a second mirror for a free third view; print a ChArUco board and a plain checkerboard; film under **bright warm LED light** (not dim dusk light — 120-240 fps phone video needs light, and captive geckos walk under lights) at 120 fps first, 240 only if exposure allows. Also film 5-10 cricket strikes for strike distance and approach speed.
- Define the ~22-keypoint schema (snout, both eyes, shoulder/elbow/wrist/manus, hip/knee/ankle/pes, girdle centres, vent, 3-5 tail points) after Jagnandan & Higham 2017 and Animal Kingdom's 23-point layout.
- Download the free fallbacks now: Chong et al. 2022 Zenodo lizard videos and XMAPortal BROWN42/BROWN38.

Track B (laptop):
- Move `"privileged"` out of the runtime obs dict in `envs/gecko_brain_env.py`; keep `oracle_action()` for teachers; add `tests/test_no_privileged_obs.py`.
- Write `rewards/realism_metrics.py` and score V4.5B (expected to fail on speed range).
- Write `eval/ethogram.py` and run it on the current brain + V4.5B; add mechanical-work logging to `envs/gecko_walk_env.py` and record joules per metre for V4.5B over 20 episodes.
- Benchmark control-steps/s: one core on the laptop; then 2 h of c7i.2xlarge spot **on Linux** (the repo's `train_brain.py` only selects `SubprocVecEnv` on Linux, so the laptop number will not size AWS workers).
- **The float32 check** (~6 h g4dn.xlarge spot, ~$2): MuJoCo Warp is float32 with no double option, is non-deterministic on GPU, clamps the solver tolerance to 1e-6 (CPU default 1e-8), and has open maintainer-acknowledged NaN issues on exactly our regime — tiny inertias plus stiff contacts (mujoco_warp issues #1000, #1415, #1517; a C. elegans imitation model already went NaN on Warp only). So it **cannot** reproduce the CPU gait trajectory-for-trajectory; the only question is whether it reproduces it qualitatively. On a fresh box install MuJoCo 3.12 + mujoco-warp (the repo pins `mujoco==3.9.0`; this is a separate environment), set the CPU model's tolerance to 1e-6 so both backends solve to the same criterion, load `gecko_body_r.xml` with `mjx.put_model(m, impl='warp')` at 1,000 worlds, replay V4.5B's exact CPU control sequence for full episodes, and compare NaN rate, foot-contact timing, CoM path, gait period and episode return against CPU; run once more with `wp.set_device("cpu")` to separate float32 error from GPU non-determinism; time the model with `mjwarp-testspeed`. If it NaNs, first try the cheap mitigations (armature / `boundinertia` on the toe bodies, sliding friction ≥ 1e-2, modest impratio) and re-run. Confirm S3 checkpoint sync and spot-resume work on the same box.
- Write `docs/brain_regions.md` (module I/O contracts, the lesion prediction table) and `config/proxies.yaml`.

**Gate (pass/fail):** (a) animal access confirmed *or* Plan B declared; (b) first test film in hand with at least 5 clean strides visible in both views, and the mirror view detects a flipped calibration board; (c) runtime obs contains no privileged key and the test passes; (d) V4.5B scorecard and ethogram baseline committed; (e) measured control-steps/s on laptop, c7i and (if it ran) Warp recorded; (f) **Warp passes only if** zero NaNs over 1,000 worlds × full episodes, foot-contact order preserved, gait period within 10% of CPU, and episode return within the spread of CPU seeds — otherwise Warp is marked "failed", the CPU fallback budget is activated, and the imitation trainers move to SB3/LocoMuJoCo on CPU. Either way, CPU double precision remains the reference physics and every checkpoint is selected on CPU.

### Phase 1 — Data, retarget, living world (Weeks 3-4) — budget ~$25

Track A:
- Collect **4-8 minutes of clean tracked walking** (straight, turning, start/stop, slow stalk) over several short sessions — that is roughly 50-100 five-second clips and several hundred strides. This is the MIMIC-MJX finding read honestly: most of the held-out gain came within ~50 clips, but their curve was still rising at 169; 20-50 strides would sit in the worst region of their curve. A walking-only gecko set is lower-entropy than rat walk/rear/groom, so 4 min is the floor and 8 min the target.
- Label 200-400 frames in DeepLabCut 3 (SuperAnimal transfer learning) or SLEAP; ~10 h of g4dn spot if laptop CPU training is too slow (~$3).
- Calibrate in Anipose with the mirror view cropped and horizontally flipped as camera 2 (aniposelib ≥ 0.4.3; the board must be co-visible in both views in the same frames; ArUco IDs only decode after the flip); triangulate with limb-length constraints. Because Anipose's camera model fixes the principal point at the crop centre, expect bias on the mirror view — so validate on physical scale (known board edge and a fixed limb-segment length in mm) and on hand-labelled frames, not only on pixel reprojection error, which one published prism/mirror attempt showed can be sub-pixel while the calibration is wrong.
- Add keypoint sites, shoulder rotation joint, inertia floors, passive damping/stiffness and activation filters to a branch copy of the MJCF (`morphology/gecko_body_r_v2.xml`); retarget with mink (or a STAC-style fit that also solves marker offsets); inspect per-limb residuals; replay retargeted `qpos` open-loop through the position actuators (Karakasiliotis 2016 test).

Track B:
- `world/prey.py` cricket FSM + mealworm class, with a unit test asserting 1,000 sampled escapes match the distributions in Section 3 (latency within ±40 ms of the mean, heading within ±30° of anti-threat, dash 28-43 mm, jump fraction 0.2-0.55 at high threat speed, response probability rising with threat speed); run the **old** proximity-eating oracle against crickets as the "prey is now evasive" check.
- `world/arena.py` (enclosure + shelter), two eye cameras with dim/noisy rendering, `envs/strike_primitive.py` with the strike window, gaze channels driving the four neck/head actuators (V4.5B keeps its CPG outputs for them until the new walker lands — gaze is exercised only on the new walker; on V4.5B the compatibility shim ignores gaze).
- `brain/basal_ganglia.py` (GPR) with hand-set saliences and dithering/persistence unit tests; `physiology/gecko_physiology.py` with drive-reduction reward, mechanical-work energy and the odour-gated fear module fitted on the laptop to the Frýdlová 2026 per-trial CSVs, old ODE selectable for regression.

**Gate:** Track A — calibration mean reprojection error **< 3 px (ideally < 1 px)** in Anipose's own units; on 50 hand-labelled validation frames, 3-D limb-point error < 12 px on 75% of frames and a median of **≤ 5 mm** measured (a target to be measured, not an Anipose capability); known-length checks within 5%; at least **4 min** of retained tracked walking; median per-limb retargeting residual under ~3 mm after body changes; open-loop replay produces forward progress with the recorded footfall order. Track B — cricket unit test passes; the old oracle's capture success on crickets falls below 50%; the GPR animal, under identical drives, survives at least as long as the if-chain arbiter with fewer than 2 channel switches per simulated minute; fear module reproduces the published pattern (odour-only overt defence in the 20-33% band, vision-only at control level).

**Plan B (if no gecko):** Track A becomes a literature-target spine: tune `spine/reticulospinal_cpg.py` gains by CMA-ES on `realism_metrics.py` using published gecko numbers only (Zaaf 2001 speed range with frequency-led speed scaling; Jagnandan & Higham 2017 duty factors and excursions; one tail cycle per stride from the 2018 paper; Chong 2022 standing wave), then an AMP style prior from DLC-labelled Chong 2022 videos of fully-limbed lizards, **explicitly labelled "sprawling-lizard style, not gecko kinematics"** in every report. The Phase 2 manifold gate is dropped in Plan B. If the gecko is filmed but fewer than 2 min survive tracking, use Plan B's style prior on top of whatever real clips exist and label the mix.

### Phase 2 — Imitation walker and surrogate brain (Weeks 5-8) — budget ~$190

Track A (GPU):
- Implement `spine/reticulospinal_cpg.py`; tune its gains by CMA-ES against the scorecard on a c7i.8xlarge spot box (not the laptop — a few thousand multi-second evaluations is days of laptop time).
- Train DeepMimic-style tracking on the clips with the CPG as action prior (LocoMuJoCo's JAX trainer on g5.xlarge with `impl='warp'` plus domain randomisation over friction, mass and motor gain, with checkpoints re-scored on CPU; or SB3 PPO on c7i.8xlarge if Warp failed the Phase 0 gate); reference-state initialisation and early termination.
- Train AMP: discriminator on state transitions from the clips (5-16 s of clean loopable strides plus turning clips, per-clip weights); task reward = following (drive_L, drive_R); reference-state initialisation from the same clips; gradient penalty and style/task mix from AMP_for_hardware; monitor the scorecard, not just reward.
- Relax physics only if the reference gait survives it: raise timestep toward 0.002 s (solref 0.006 allows it — 7.5× the current step, above MuJoCo's 2× rule), cut `iterations` from 100 toward 10; every relaxed setting is re-validated against CPU V4.5B first.
- Realistic run budget. The honest throughput anchor for a small contact-rich body is NeuroMechFly on MuJoCo Warp: **~105k physics steps/s at 1,000 worlds on an RTX 3080 Ti (physics only; ~53k once their CPU-side rendering is included)**, and the GPU was not saturated at 1,000 worlds. Discounting ~0.6-0.9× for an A10G and dividing by 25 substeps gives roughly **2-4k control-steps/s** before policy inference, observation and reward work — so a 1e8-step run is 7-14 h and a 3e8-step run is 20-40 h. 180 GPU hours therefore buys about **one long 3e8 tracking run plus three or four runs of 1e8-2e8** (MIMIC-MJX's rat needed ~6.5e8 steps to reach 95% of its test reward, so the tracking run gets the longest budget and the AMP runs are shorter). Not 10-15 runs. The Phase 0 `mjwarp-testspeed` number replaces this estimate the moment it exists.

Track B (laptop + CPU spot):
- `surrogate/hunt2d.py`: Malik-style 2-D arena with a sectored two-eye retina (type + distance per sector, binocular sectors less noisy), coupled turn/forward/stalk/strike actions at ~8-10 Hz, the same cricket FSM and physiology, curriculum from static → random-walk → evasive prey with a narrowing strike window. PPO + LSTM, ~4M steps per seed (hours on the laptop).
- `brain/tectum.py`: retina resample, small conv front-end trained supervised (labels at training time only) to emit the same sectored representation from the eye cameras, plus the recurrent saliency map with slow suppression (~20 h g4dn spot, ~$6).
- Transfer onto MuJoCo over V4.5B via the compatibility shim: BC/DAgger from the surrogate policy, then RecurrentPPO fine-tune with drive-reduction reward, 16-32 `SubprocVecEnv` workers on c7i.8xlarge spot, decisions at 10 Hz.

**Gate (Track A):** commanded speeds of at least **0.2-0.35 m/s** (the upper end only if the clips cover it) reached by modulating the descending drive, with most of the speed change carried by stride frequency; hind duty factor 0.77-0.78 and fore 0.70-0.72 at walking speed, decreasing with speed; joint excursions within the Jagnandan & Higham 2017 Table 1 ranges; one lateral tail cycle per stride with tail-tip amplitude decreasing with speed (the derived 1.2-2.0 Hz band is reported, not gated); DTW to held-out strides better than V4.5B by a large margin; zero falls over 60 s at 0.25 m/s on CPU physics; **≥ 90%** of frames inside the recorded kinematic manifold (MIMIC-MJX metric, relaxed from 95%). **Gate (Track B):** surrogate agent shows prey-angle reduction ≥ 40% per approach bout; MuJoCo animal over V4.5B captures ≥ 50% of evasive crickets within 60 s with pure sensing (the frozen walker cannot turn fast, so 50%, not 60-70%); no fixation on a non-prey object longer than 5 s.

### Phase 3 — Integration, senses, brain re-hookup (Weeks 9-11) — budget ~$70

- Swap the imitation walker in behind the descending-drive interface; the brain modules do not change. Retrain the brain with the existing oracle → BC → DAgger → visual-distillation ladder (teacher at training time only) with a recurrent core; the new walker's stalk (low drive) and strike now work together.
- Add the substrate-vibration channel; add the tongue-flick action reading a scalar odour field at `nose_tip` (I = I_peak/d²) **only if** weeks 9-10 are on schedule. Thermal field, diel clock, gut model and hippocampal memory are explicitly deferred to the post-3-month plan.
- Run the pre-registered lesion/ablation battery (predictions written before running): silence the tectal orient readout → capture drops, locomotion unchanged; remove vibration → fewer detections of prey behind the head; clamp the drive-reduction error → salience values stop changing; add 13% anterior mass shift (tail autotomy) → walker must not fall; remove odour (if implemented) → overt defence collapses to the 2-7% control baseline even with a visible predator, while vision still raises alertness and tongue-flick rate (the Frýdlová 2026 pattern: defence strictly gated by chemoreception, sampling additive). Note honestly that the module-silencing outcomes are largely true by construction; the evidential weight sits in the ethogram time budget and the perturbation survival.

**Gate:** ≥ 60% capture on evasive crickets with pure sensing over the new walker, with the strike launched from a stand-off rather than a walk-in; behaviour time budget with sensory exploration the largest category (real geckos with live prey: ~53% of units) and foraging under 10% (real: 4.1%); the walker survives the tail-autotomy mass shift with < 10% more falls; every lesion prediction recorded as hit or miss.

## 6. What a neuroscientist would say is real — and what it still is not

**Real, in the sense a comparative biomechanist or motor neuroscientist would accept as a model rather than a demo:**
- The gait is not designed by reward engineering; it is constrained to reproduce the owner's own 3-D-tracked leopard-gecko strides and scored against published relations for the species (speed range with frequency-led speed scaling, stride length vs speed, duty factor, joint excursions, one tail cycle per stride with amplitude falling with speed, trunk standing wave). This is the same standard the virtual rodent, MIMIC-MJX and flybody are held to.
- The spine is organised the way vertebrate spinal circuits are described (bilateral descending drive → rhythm generators with load and limb-angle feedback), and the brain talks to it through a sparse descending command, as in NeuroMechFly, the salamander models and Eon's embodied fly.
- The animal senses only through its body: two dim colour eyes, head IMU, foot and belly touch, substrate vibration, and (if reached) tongue-flick chemosense. No privileged food vector or world pose reaches any module at runtime, and a test enforces it.
- Behaviour is scored against measured leopard-gecko ethology, not against reward: the ethogram time budget, stand-off-and-lunge capture with success in the measured range, prey-angle reduction per bout, odour-gated rather than vision-led defence fitted to a public per-trial dataset, survival under a tail-autotomy mass shift.
- Eating, resting and searching are consequences of physiology (drive-reduction reward over a body that pays for movement in simulated work), selected by a published basal-ganglia model rather than an if-chain.

**Not real, stated bluntly:**
- It is not a connectome, not spiking, and not a whole-brain model. No gecko wiring or recordings exist, so nothing inside the brain is fitted to neural data; the region names (tectum, basal ganglia) describe function and sign, not measured circuits, and the lesion battery mostly confirms the architecture we built.
- The spine's structure is borrowed from salamanders, humans and robots; no element has been tested against gecko physiology.
- The body is driven by PD position actuators with activation filters, not muscles; "energy" is simulated joint work, not metabolism; forces and gains are arbitrary, so joules-per-metre is not physiology.
- Any walker trained on the GPU learned in float32 physics that does not match the double-precision reference step for step; we report it as real only after it is re-scored on CPU.
- Several sensory and motor parameters are proxies: visual field, binocular overlap and acuity (other nocturnal geckos), strike distance and speed (juvenile banded geckos at 3-4 g), vibration band (tokay gecko), tail frequency (derived from stance time and duty factor, never measured). Ground-reaction-force signatures come from a padded *Hemidactylus* trotting with adhesion undeployed and are used as a soft check only.
- The prey is a finite-state machine blended from three cricket lab paradigms, the world is a box with a shelter, and thermal biology, the day-night cycle, digestion, hydration, skin, claw mechanics, growth, memory of places and social behaviour are absent in the 3-month deliverable.
- The 3-D tracking comes from a phone and a mirror, a rig that Anipose's authors have not tested; its accuracy is whatever we measure, not a published capability.
- If Plan B is triggered, the walker's style comes from other lizards and the "gecko kinematics" claim is not made at all.

## 7. Risks and how we handle them

| Risk | Handling |
|---|---|
| No leopard gecko to film, or it freezes and yields too few strides | Decide by end of week 2; several short sessions under warm bright light; borrow from a breeder or pet shop; Plan B (literature-target spine + sprawling-lizard style prior) is fully specified and labelled honestly. Track B is unaffected. |
| Not enough clean data: the MIMIC-MJX curve says 4-8 min of tracked walking, a phone rig on a gecko may yield less | Many short sessions; count retained minutes weekly; below 4 min the tracking run still proceeds but the manifold gate is reported, not enforced; below 2 min, mix in the Plan B style prior and label it. |
| Phone + mirror rig cannot reach usable 3-D accuracy on a 5-10 cm animal | Mirror handled as Anipose's FAQ says (crop, flip, treat as camera 2), aniposelib ≥ 0.4.3, board co-visible in both views; gate on pixel reprojection, hand-labelled 3-D error and a physical-scale check, not on a mm figure nobody has published; second mirror for a third view; fall back to a second phone triggered by a clap for sync; 120 fps before 240. |
| Forelimb DoF mismatch: the MJCF cannot fit real shoulder motion | Per-limb residual gate in Phase 1; add shoulder rotation and, if needed, a passive foot pitch; change the body rather than bend the data. |
| float32 MuJoCo Warp NaNs or drifts on the tiny-mass (0.2 g geoms), ±0.4 N body — an open issue class in the Warp tracker | The Phase 0 six-hour A/B (CPU control replay, NaN rate, contact timing, CoM path, `wp.set_device("cpu")` to isolate non-determinism, matched 1e-6 tolerance) decides before any long run; inertia floors, friction ≥ 1e-2 and modest impratio are tried first; if it still fails, all imitation training moves to SB3/LocoMuJoCo on c7i.8xlarge from the $100 earmarked reserve. CPU double stays the reference physics and every checkpoint is selected on CPU either way. |
| Throughput lower than planned; too few imitation runs | Budget is sized for 2-4k control-steps/s (physics-only anchor ~105k steps/s on a 3080 Ti, discounted for A10G and RL overhead) and for one long run plus three or four short ones; `mjwarp-testspeed` on the real model in Phase 0 replaces the estimate; relax timestep/iterations only after CPU re-validation; DeepMimic tracking first so a usable walker exists before any AMP run. |
| AMP discriminator collapse or a single-gait mode collapse | DeepMimic baseline first; 5-16 s of loopable clips with per-clip weights and reference-state initialisation, as the released AMP_for_hardware code actually does; gradient penalty and style/task mix from that repo; CPG kept as the action mean; monitor the scorecard, not reward; keep V4.5B as the fallback walker. |
| Brain training bound by per-step camera rendering (current configs use `num_envs=1`, `DummyVecEnv`) | Decisions at 10 Hz with rendering only on decision steps; most policy learning in the 2-D surrogate on the laptop; 16-32 `SubprocVecEnv` workers on Linux c7i spot; benchmark before committing hours. |
| Capture fails for motor reasons on V4.5B (0.11 m/s, no fast turning) versus crickets that dash 28-43 mm in ~0.3 s | The strike primitive, not walking speed, covers the last stand-off distance; Track B gates over V4.5B are set at 50%, rising to 60% only over the imitation walker. |
| Scripted crickets get exploited (agent learns the FSM rather than hunting) | Stochastic latency, heading error and run/jump draws in every escape; Tsutsui-style learned evader as the swap-in if capture rate exceeds the ~83% measured for real geckos. |
| Degenerate homeostasis (parks and starves) or dithering under the GPR selector | Girard 2003 salience forms and dithering/persistence unit tests; curriculum from the surrogate; per-drive value heads only after the hand-set selector is stable. |
| Spot interruptions (> 20% monthly frequency on g5 and c7i in us-east-1) and zone price spread | Checkpoint every 100k steps with resume flags; capacity-optimised multi-AZ fleet; no zone-pinned volumes; budget sized at the mean with the worst zone noted. |
| Scope creep: thermal, diel, chemosense, hippocampus, cerebellum, muscles all at once | Explicit post-3-month list; sensory items ordered by realism-per-hour (crickets and two eyes first, vibration, chemosense last); the hard rules above. |
| Trained weights lost again | S3 checkpoint every 100k steps, spot-resume flags, `models/INVENTORY.md`, weekly sync to the laptop. |
| Attachment to V4.5B as "final" | It stays in the repo as the regression baseline and is replaced only when the new walker beats it on realism and matches it on falls, side by side in the report. |

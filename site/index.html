Why it is not a gecko-shaped robot

It reproduces experiments on real geckos that nobody fitted it to.

Geckos walked over force plates so their legs could be weighed. Geckos were filmed at high speed eating crickets. Geckos slept with electrodes under their eyelids. Geckos were shown a live snake, to find out what actually frightens them — it is smell, not sight.

How it was built

Assembled, not trained.

The virtual fly and the virtual rat are trained neural networks. This animal is arithmetic: a hand-written spinal rhythm on measured gait numbers, a published basal-ganglia model that reproduces its paper’s table to four decimal places, hunger computed from its own metabolism. When a network was trained for six million steps to improve the walk, it came out worse, and was thrown away. That is why it can run here at all: there are no weights to download.

The browser build is checked, not trusted. tools/conformance_web.py runs the JavaScript and the original Python on the same inputs and compares them: the walker agrees to 7.3e−15 across all thirty actuators, and the basal ganglia and the clock agree exactly. It is the same animal, not a lookalike — and that test caught a real bug before you saw it.

What it cannot do

The rest of this page is only worth reading if this part is honest.

  • About half built. Five of eight brain modules exist. Memory and learning do not.
  • It mostly misses. It stalks, strikes with a real jaw, and catches on roughly 6.5% of strikes.
  • The eye is the weak link, and is not running on this page — here the animal has hunger, temperature and a clock, not vision.
  • The citations are not yet verified. 212 references harvested from the project’s own text; none checked against a bibliographic database.
  • Two of six gait gates fail, and the animal does not grow, age or reproduce.

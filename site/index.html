      ? What guided means
      On — four things are given to the animal, and this is all of them: it knows where the cricket is inside the arena; the commitment is handed over rather than accumulated; the cricket does not bolt; and wanting it outranks everything else. The physics, the walk, the body and the 4.07 cm it has to close are untouched — it still gets there on its own legs.

      Off — the animal as it is. Measured over 3000 steps: the cricket was in view on 215 frames, the eye fired on 487, and 18 of those overlapped. It hunts by persistence, not acuity, and catches are rare.

      Nothing in the lab model knows this switch exists. Every number quoted here was measured with it off.

      What this is

      A virtual leopard gecko, built out of real biology.

      Not an animation of a gecko. A body whose mass, proportions and joint travel come from measurements of living Eublepharis macularius, carried by a physics engine at 500 steps a second, driven by a brain whose parts are models of real ones — and every number in it is labelled with where it came from.

      What you are looking at

      The top panel is the animal in a patch of desert. Everything in it is being solved, not played back: the feet load and slip, the tail swings because it is a fifth of the body's mass, the skin is deformed by the bones underneath it.

      The bottom panel is the same animal at the same instant, with the skin taken off. The pale shapes are the 48 collision solids the physics actually integrates — not a drawing of a skeleton, the real thing the engine uses. The thin blue lines are 27 nerve fibres, one per driven joint, each routed along the animal's own body tree: out of the head, down through the girdle, into the limb, ending at that joint's true anchor. A dot travelling one of them is a single motor command.

      The brain

      Six behaviours compete: hunt, flee, explore, bask, rest, groom. They do not take turns and they are not scripted. Each one puts forward how much it wants to run, and a model of the basal ganglia — nine nuclei, run to a fixed point every control step — holds all six down and releases exactly one. A channel that cannot get its salience above the measured release floor of 0.1999 never runs, however far ahead of its rivals it is.

      What drives them is the animal's own state. A day/night clock winds arousal down and rest takes over. Lying on cool ground drops its body temperature and bask wins, which walks it to a warm patch and then makes it lie down — because this species takes heat from the ground, not from light. Being warm and hungry with nothing in sight releases explore, which is not walking in a straight line: it stops, sweeps its head in 22° jumps, turns its body, walks a little, and scans again.

      The eye

      A camera rides the animal's snout at the position the morphology specifies. Every control step it renders a frame, and a model of the retina and optic tectum looks for a cricket in those pixels. The red channel is thrown away first, because this animal's longest-wavelength pigment sits near 521 nm. What survives is temporal contrast — what moved — measured against what the animal's own walking should have made move.

      Nothing is told where the cricket is. The animal finds it in its own rendered pixels or it does not find it.

      That matters, because the easy version of this page would hand the brain a coordinate and let it walk at it, and then the hunt would prove nothing. There is a switch on the live page that does exactly that, clearly labelled, because the honest detector is not good: over 3000 steps the cricket was in view on 215 frames, the eye fired on 487, and 18 of those overlapped. It hunts by persistence rather than by acuity.

      Every number says where it came from

      The rule this project runs on is that a value is published, derived or invented, and it says which, next to itself, in the source. A plausible number wearing a published number's clothes is the worst defect available. These are the ones the live page is standing on:

      QuantityValueProvenance

      Invented does not mean guessed and hidden — it means nobody has measured it and the model says so out loud. The leg rhythm is a case in point: no stride frequency has ever been published for this species, so the number carries the label rather than a citation it does not have.

      What it cannot do

      flee has nowhere to go: this world has no predator and no refuge, so the channel sits at zero rather than being given something to run from. groom is a flat zero too — grooming in this species has a published trigger, licking the lips after a meal, and that is a reflex elsewhere rather than something this selector competes for. Both are honestly unreachable rather than quietly propped up.

      The detector is weak, and the page says so on its own face. The strike is not wired to the capture. The energetics are not ported. This is the current state of a thing that is still being built, and the ledger behind it — every hypothesis, with the refutations kept — is in the repository.

      Contact

      Send a message. It comes straight to me.

      • It gets hungry. On the interval a real one does — 2.33 days between meals, which is a measured number, not a timer I picked.
      • It gets cold and goes to warm up. By lying on warm ground, because that is how this species actually takes heat — not from a lamp.
      • It sleeps when it should. Its own day/night clock winds it down; a leopard gecko is not awake at noon and neither is this one.
      • It hunts for itself. Its eye has to find the cricket in what it is actually looking at. Nothing hands it a position.
      • You can feed it, warm it, and pick it up — and watch which of the six channels in its basal ganglia wins while you do.

      None of that is a loop pretending to be an animal. It is the same simulated biology you just watched, living on your machine instead of mine. It does not exist yet — this page is the engine it would run on. Tell me you want one and I will tell you when it is real.
